# Federal CMV Record-Retention and Preservation Matrix

An attorney-edited reference to selected federal minimum retention periods for records that may matter in commercial-motor-vehicle litigation.

## The key distinction

A **regulatory retention period** is not the same thing as a **litigation preservation deadline**.

The federal rules require covered entities to keep defined records for specified minimum periods. They do not guarantee that every camera clip, telematics event, message, safety alert, portal record, engine/vehicle artifact, or vendor log will survive for that period. Once litigation is reasonably anticipated, preservation duties and court orders may affect routine destruction. Case-specific advice and prompt action are necessary.

## Included federal records

- driver records of duty status and supporting documents;
- separate ELD backup records;
- motor-carrier accident register;
- driver qualification files and shorter-lived components;
- vehicle inspection, repair, and maintenance records;
- driver vehicle inspection reports (DVIRs) prepared under the applicable rule;
- periodic inspection reports;
- entry-level driver training-provider records.

## Important exclusions

This draft is not a complete FMCSR retention schedule. It does not yet map every controlled-substances/alcohol testing record, Clearinghouse record, drug-testing consortium record, dispatch/business record, cargo document, toll/fuel record, broker record, insurance file, hazmat record, camera system, telematics platform, OEM diagnostic artifact, state rule, or company policy.

## How to use the CSV responsibly

- Read the current linked regulation and confirm applicability, definitions, exceptions, and effective date.
- Preserve the exact eCFR access date or point-in-time text used.
- Distinguish records required by regulation from additional records the carrier or vendor actually creates.
- Request native data, metadata, audit history, attachments, surrounding time, and custodian/account information where appropriate.
- Do not tell a client or investigator that it is safe to wait until the regulatory period is near expiration.
